What is Orthodontics?

Orthodontics is the branch of dentistry concerned with the diagnosis, prevention and management of malpositioned teeth and certain developing dentofacial irregularities.

Orthodontic treatment aims to improve dental relationships, occlusion and, when appropriate, facial and functional considerations through carefully planned treatment.

For BDS students: A good understanding of craniofacial growth, dental development, occlusion, malocclusion and orthodontic diagnosis is essential for learning orthodontics.

Malocclusion can result from interactions between genetic, skeletal, dental, functional and environmental factors.

Orthodontic Diagnosis

Orthodontic diagnosis involves systematic collection and interpretation of clinical and diagnostic information.

General Diagnostic Approach

  1. Patient history
  2. Chief complaint
  3. Extraoral examination
  4. Facial profile assessment
  5. Intraoral examination
  6. Occlusal assessment
  7. Dental casts or digital models when indicated
  8. Clinical photographs
  9. Radiographic assessment when indicated
  10. Cephalometric analysis when required
  11. Diagnosis
  12. Treatment planning

Orthodontic Treatment Planning

Treatment planning should be based on the patient's diagnosis, growth status, dental and skeletal relationships, oral health, treatment goals and individual needs.

  1. Identify the patient's concerns.
  2. Complete clinical examination.
  3. Establish the diagnosis.
  4. Assess growth and development.
  5. Evaluate dental and skeletal relationships.
  6. Determine treatment objectives.
  7. Consider appropriate treatment options.
  8. Discuss expected benefits and limitations.
  9. Plan retention and follow-up.

Orthodontics Viva Questions

What is orthodontics?

Orthodontics is the branch of dentistry concerned with the diagnosis, prevention and management of malpositioned teeth and selected dentofacial irregularities.

What is malocclusion?

Malocclusion refers to an abnormal relationship between teeth or between the dental arches.

What is overjet?

Overjet describes the horizontal relationship between opposing anterior teeth.

What is overbite?

Overbite describes the vertical overlap of the anterior teeth.

What is anchorage?

Anchorage refers to resistance to unwanted tooth movement during orthodontic treatment.

What is cephalometry?

Cephalometry is the measurement and analysis of skeletal, dental and soft tissue relationships using standardized cephalometric radiographs.

Patient Education

Patients undergoing orthodontic treatment need good oral hygiene, regular appointments and appropriate care of their orthodontic appliances.

  • Maintain thorough oral hygiene.
  • Follow the orthodontist's appliance-care instructions.
  • Attend scheduled appointments.
  • Report broken or loose appliances.
  • Follow dietary instructions when required.
  • Use removable appliances and retainers as instructed.
  • Continue regular dental check-ups.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are braces?

Braces are orthodontic appliances that use components attached to teeth to apply controlled forces and assist planned tooth movement.

What is interceptive orthodontics?

Interceptive orthodontics involves early recognition and management of selected developing orthodontic problems.

Why are retainers used after orthodontic treatment?

Retainers are used to help maintain the achieved tooth positions after active orthodontic treatment.

What is orthodontic treatment planning?

Orthodontic treatment planning involves developing an appropriate treatment approach based on diagnosis, treatment objectives and individual patient factors.

Can children need orthodontic assessment?

Orthodontic assessment may be useful during childhood when there are concerns involving dental development, eruption, occlusion or developing jaw relationships.
