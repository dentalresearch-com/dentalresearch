Oral Pathology

Learn oral diseases, oral lesions, odontogenic cysts and tumors, salivary gland disorders, oral cancer, potentially malignant disorders, jaw lesions, biopsy and histopathology.

What is Oral Pathology?

Oral Pathology is the branch of dentistry concerned with the study of diseases affecting the oral cavity, jaws, salivary glands and related structures.

It combines clinical examination, radiographic findings, laboratory investigations and microscopic examination to understand the nature, diagnosis and behavior of oral diseases.

Oral pathology is an important subject in dentistry because recognition of abnormal oral findings can help in early diagnosis, appropriate investigation and timely referral.

Clinical Approach to Oral Lesions

Evaluation of an oral lesion begins with systematic clinical assessment. Important information includes the history, duration, location, appearance, symptoms and progression of the lesion.

Important Steps

  • Detailed medical and dental history
  • History of the presenting lesion
  • Extraoral examination
  • Intraoral examination
  • Inspection and palpation
  • Assessment of lesion size and location
  • Assessment of color and surface characteristics
  • Evaluation of consistency and tenderness
  • Regional lymph node examination when indicated
  • Selection of appropriate investigations

White Oral Lesions

White oral lesions may result from increased keratin production, epithelial thickening, edema, surface changes or other pathological processes.

Clinical evaluation should consider whether the lesion can be removed by gentle wiping, its distribution, duration, associated symptoms and risk factors.

Examples

  • Leukoplakia
  • Leukoedema
  • Oral lichen planus
  • Frictional keratosis
  • White lesions associated with infections
  • Hereditary white mucosal conditions

Red Oral Lesions

Red lesions may occur because of epithelial thinning, inflammation, vascular changes, trauma or neoplastic processes.

Persistent unexplained red lesions require careful assessment because some red mucosal lesions may have important clinical significance.

Examples

  • Erythroplakia
  • Inflammatory lesions
  • Traumatic lesions
  • Vascular lesions
  • Atrophic mucosal conditions

Oral Potentially Malignant Disorders

Oral potentially malignant disorders are conditions associated with an increased risk of developing oral cancer compared with clinically normal oral mucosa.

Early recognition, elimination of modifiable risk factors, appropriate investigation and regular follow-up are important components of management.

Clinical reminder: A persistent unexplained oral lesion should be evaluated by a qualified dental or medical professional rather than self-diagnosed.

Oral Cancer

Oral cancer can affect different structures of the oral cavity and may present as a persistent ulcer, growth, induration, unexplained bleeding, change in mucosal color or other persistent abnormality.

Important Risk Factors

  • Tobacco exposure
  • Alcohol exposure
  • Betel quid and areca nut use
  • Some viral infections
  • Chronic environmental and behavioral risk factors

Early recognition and appropriate diagnostic evaluation are important because delayed diagnosis can affect treatment options and outcomes.

Odontogenic Cysts

Odontogenic cysts are pathological cavities associated with tissues involved in tooth development. They may be inflammatory or developmental in origin.

Important Examples

  • Radicular cyst
  • Residual cyst
  • Dentigerous cyst
  • Odontogenic keratocyst
  • Lateral periodontal cyst
  • Eruption cyst

Diagnosis may require correlation of clinical findings, radiographic appearance and histopathological examination.

Odontogenic Tumors

Odontogenic tumors arise from tissues associated with tooth development. They vary widely in biological behavior and clinical presentation.

Examples

  • Ameloblastoma
  • Odontoma
  • Adenomatoid odontogenic tumor
  • Calcifying odontogenic tumors
  • Other odontogenic neoplasms

Radiographic characteristics, clinical behavior and histopathology help establish an accurate diagnosis.

Salivary Gland Pathology

Salivary gland diseases include inflammatory, obstructive, cystic, benign neoplastic and malignant conditions.

Commonly Studied Conditions

  • Sialadenitis
  • Sialolithiasis
  • Mucocele
  • Ranula
  • Pleomorphic adenoma
  • Mucoepidermoid carcinoma
  • Adenoid cystic carcinoma

Jaw and Bone Lesions

Lesions of the jaws may be odontogenic, non-odontogenic, inflammatory, fibro-osseous, developmental or neoplastic.

Clinical examination and imaging are important in identifying the location, extent and characteristics of a jaw lesion. Histopathological examination may be required for definitive diagnosis.

Biopsy in Oral Pathology

A biopsy involves obtaining tissue for microscopic examination. It is an important diagnostic procedure for many persistent, suspicious or unexplained oral lesions.

Types of Biopsy

  • Incisional biopsy
  • Excisional biopsy
  • Punch biopsy
  • Fine-needle aspiration in selected situations

The choice of procedure depends on lesion size, location, clinical appearance, suspected diagnosis and the purpose of the investigation.

Histopathology

Histopathology involves microscopic examination of tissues to identify structural and cellular changes associated with disease.

Important Histological Concepts

  • Inflammation
  • Cellular injury
  • Edema
  • Hyperplasia
  • Dysplasia
  • Neoplasia
  • Necrosis
  • Fibrosis

Patient Education

Patients should seek professional evaluation for oral abnormalities that persist, recur or change over time.

  • Do not ignore a persistent oral ulcer.
  • Report unexplained oral bleeding or swelling.
  • Seek evaluation for persistent white or red patches.
  • Avoid tobacco and areca nut products.
  • Attend regular dental examinations.
  • Follow professional recommendations for investigations and follow-up.

Frequently Asked Questions About Oral Pathology

What does an oral pathologist study?

Oral pathology focuses on diseases affecting the oral cavity, jaws, salivary glands and related tissues.

What is an oral lesion?

An oral lesion is an abnormal change in the appearance or structure of oral tissues. It may have many possible causes and requires clinical assessment when persistent or suspicious.

What is a biopsy?

A biopsy is a procedure in which tissue is obtained for microscopic examination to assist in diagnosis.

What is oral cancer?

Oral cancer refers to malignant tumors arising in tissues of the oral cavity. Early professional evaluation of suspicious lesions is important.

Can an oral white patch be cancer?

A white patch can have many causes, and appearance alone cannot establish a diagnosis. Persistent or unexplained lesions should be examined by a qualified healthcare professional.
