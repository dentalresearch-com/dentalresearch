Oral Hygiene

Oral hygiene refers to the daily practices used to maintain cleanliness of the teeth, gingiva, tongue and other structures of the oral cavity and to prevent oral disease.

What Is Oral Hygiene?

Oral hygiene is the regular removal and control of dental plaque and other deposits from the oral cavity. Effective oral hygiene helps maintain healthy teeth and periodontal tissues and reduces the risk of dental caries, gingivitis and periodontal disease.

Key concept: Dental plaque is a major modifiable factor in the development of dental caries and plaque-induced periodontal inflammation.

Why Is Oral Hygiene Important?

Controls Plaque

Regular mechanical plaque removal reduces the accumulation of bacterial biofilm on tooth surfaces.

Prevents Gingivitis

Effective plaque control helps prevent inflammation of the gingival tissues.

Reduces Caries Risk

Plaque control together with fluoride exposure helps reduce the risk of dental caries.

Protects Periodontal Health

Maintaining good plaque control is an important component of periodontal disease prevention and maintenance.

Tooth Brushing

Tooth brushing is one of the most important methods of mechanical plaque control.

Recommended approach

  1. Use a soft-bristled toothbrush.
  2. Use fluoride-containing toothpaste appropriate for the individual.
  3. Clean all accessible tooth surfaces systematically.
  4. Brush the gingival margin gently.
  5. Avoid excessive force that may contribute to gingival trauma or tooth abrasion.
Clinical point: Brushing technique is more important than aggressive brushing. The aim is effective plaque removal without damaging the teeth or gingiva.

Tooth Brushing Techniques

Technique Main feature Clinical relevance
Modified Bass Bristles are directed toward the gingival margin and small vibratory movements are used. Commonly recommended for effective plaque control at the gingival margin.
Stillman Bristles are positioned partly on the cervical tooth surface and gingiva. Can be useful for gingival stimulation and cervical plaque control.
Charters Bristles are directed toward the occlusal or incisal surface. May be useful around orthodontic appliances and certain interdental areas.

Fluoride Toothpaste

Fluoride toothpaste provides topical fluoride during brushing and plays an important role in prevention and control of dental caries.

  • Use an appropriate fluoride toothpaste regularly.
  • Follow age-appropriate recommendations for children.
  • Avoid unnecessary rinsing immediately after brushing when appropriate, so that fluoride can remain available in the oral cavity.
Important: Toothpaste should be selected according to age, caries risk and individual clinical needs.

Interdental Cleaning

Tooth brushing alone does not adequately clean every interdental surface. Interdental cleaning should therefore be included when appropriate.

Dental Floss

Useful for cleaning tight interdental contacts where floss can be introduced safely.

Interdental Brushes

Particularly useful when interdental spaces are sufficiently large to accommodate a brush.

Periodontal point: The appropriate interdental cleaning method should be selected according to interdental anatomy, periodontal status and patient ability.

Mouthwash

Mouthrinses may be used as an adjunct to mechanical plaque control in selected individuals. They should not be considered a replacement for tooth brushing and interdental cleaning.

Common categories

  • Fluoride mouthrinses
  • Antiseptic mouthrinses
  • Essential-oil-based mouthrinses
  • Other therapeutic formulations
Remember: Mouthwash is an adjunct, not a substitute for mechanical plaque removal.

Tongue Cleaning

The tongue can retain microorganisms and debris. Gentle tongue cleaning may help reduce coating and improve oral cleanliness.

A toothbrush or tongue-cleaning device may be used according to individual preference and tolerance.

Diet and Oral Health

Oral hygiene should be combined with appropriate dietary habits to reduce the risk of oral disease.

  • Reduce frequent consumption of fermentable carbohydrates.
  • Avoid repeated sugary snacks and drinks throughout the day.
  • Prefer a balanced diet.
  • Maintain adequate hydration.

Oral Hygiene and Gingivitis

Dental plaque accumulation at the gingival margin can produce an inflammatory response in susceptible individuals.

Improving plaque control can reduce gingival inflammation. Persistent bleeding, swelling or other periodontal symptoms should be clinically evaluated rather than managed only by increasing brushing force.

Common Oral Hygiene Mistakes

  • Brushing too aggressively.
  • Using a worn-out toothbrush for too long.
  • Ignoring interdental areas.
  • Cleaning only the front surfaces of the teeth.
  • Using mouthwash as a replacement for brushing.
  • Frequent sugary snacking.
  • Ignoring persistent gingival bleeding.

Daily Oral Hygiene Routine

Morning: Brush thoroughly with fluoride toothpaste.

During the day: Maintain sensible dietary and hydration habits.

Evening: Brush again and perform appropriate interdental cleaning.

Regularly: Attend dental examinations and professional preventive care according to individual risk.

BDS Quick Revision

  • Primary aim: Control dental plaque and maintain oral cleanliness.
  • Main method: Mechanical plaque removal.
  • Toothbrush: Soft bristles are generally preferred.
  • Toothpaste: Fluoride toothpaste helps prevent dental caries.
  • Interdental cleaning: Floss or interdental brushes may be selected according to anatomy.
  • Mouthwash: An adjunct, not a replacement for mechanical cleaning.
  • Clinical objective: Prevent caries, gingivitis and periodontal disease.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I brush my teeth?

Brushing twice daily with fluoride toothpaste is commonly recommended for effective routine plaque control.

Is brushing alone enough?

No. Brushing may not adequately clean interdental areas, so an appropriate interdental cleaning method should be used when indicated.

Is mouthwash necessary?

Mouthwash is not essential for everyone. Certain formulations may provide additional benefits in selected situations, but they do not replace brushing and interdental cleaning.

Why do my gums bleed while brushing?

Gingival bleeding can be associated with plaque-induced inflammation, but persistent bleeding should be assessed clinically to determine the cause.
