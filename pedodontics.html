Learn pediatric dentistry, child oral health, primary and mixed dentition, dental caries in children, preventive dentistry, pulp therapy, space maintainers, dental trauma and behavior management.

What is Pedodontics?

Pedodontics, also known as Pediatric Dentistry, is the branch of dentistry concerned with the oral health care of infants, children and adolescents. It focuses on prevention, diagnosis and management of oral and dental conditions during growth and development.

Pediatric dental care includes preventive dentistry, management of dental caries, pulp therapy, treatment of developing dentition, dental trauma, orthodontic considerations and guidance for parents and children.

Pediatric Dental Assessment

Pediatric dental assessment should consider the child's age, development, medical history, dental history, dietary habits, oral hygiene and behavior.

Important Components

  • Medical and dental history
  • Assessment of oral hygiene
  • Dietary history
  • Dental caries risk assessment
  • Assessment of tooth eruption
  • Evaluation of occlusion and developing dentition
  • Soft tissue examination
  • Radiographic examination when indicated
  • Assessment of child behavior

Primary and Mixed Dentition

Understanding primary and mixed dentition is essential in pediatric dentistry because dental treatment must consider the developing permanent dentition.

Primary Dentition

Primary teeth contribute to mastication, speech, aesthetics and maintenance of arch space. Premature loss of primary teeth may influence eruption and space relationships.

Mixed Dentition

Mixed dentition is the developmental stage in which both primary and permanent teeth are present. Monitoring eruption and space relationships is important during this period.

Dental Caries in Children

Dental caries is one of the most common oral health problems affecting children. Caries development is influenced by dental biofilm, fermentable carbohydrates, susceptible tooth surfaces, time and modifying factors such as saliva and fluoride exposure.

Risk Factors

  • Frequent intake of sugary foods and drinks
  • Poor oral hygiene
  • Inadequate fluoride exposure
  • Previous history of dental caries
  • High-risk dietary patterns
  • Reduced protective effects of saliva

Prevention

  • Effective tooth brushing with appropriate fluoride toothpaste
  • Dietary counseling
  • Fluoride use according to individual needs
  • Pit and fissure sealants when indicated
  • Regular dental visits
  • Early identification of high-risk children

Early Childhood Caries

Early Childhood Caries (ECC) refers to dental caries occurring in young children. It can progress rapidly and may affect feeding, sleep, comfort and quality of life.

Prevention begins early through parental education, appropriate feeding practices, oral hygiene and regular dental assessment.

Key point: Early preventive dental visits can help identify caries risk before extensive disease develops.

Preventive Dentistry in Children

Fluoride

Fluoride helps reduce the risk of dental caries by supporting remineralization and improving resistance of tooth structure to acid dissolution.

Pit and Fissure Sealants

Sealants can provide a protective barrier over susceptible pits and fissures, particularly in teeth at increased risk of occlusal caries.

Oral Hygiene Education

Parents and children should receive age-appropriate guidance regarding tooth brushing, fluoride toothpaste, diet and regular dental care.

Pediatric Pulp Therapy

Pulp therapy aims to manage pulpal disease while preserving the function of primary teeth when appropriate.

Pulpotomy

Pulpotomy involves removal of the affected coronal pulp while attempting to maintain healthy radicular pulp tissue.

Pulpectomy

Pulpectomy involves removal of the pulp tissue from the root canal system of a primary tooth when indicated.

Treatment selection depends on clinical findings, radiographic findings, restorability, tooth development and the child's overall circumstances.

Space Maintainers

Premature loss of a primary tooth may result in changes in the developing dentition. Space maintainers are appliances designed to preserve available space when clinically indicated.

Common Types

  • Band and loop space maintainer
  • Distal shoe
  • Lingual arch
  • Nance appliance
  • Transpalatal arch

Selection depends on the tooth lost, location, eruption status of permanent teeth, arch characteristics and the child's clinical needs.

Dental Trauma in Children

Dental trauma may involve primary or permanent teeth and can affect teeth, periodontal tissues and supporting structures.

Important Considerations

  • Type and extent of injury
  • Stage of tooth development
  • Presence of mobility or displacement
  • Soft tissue injuries
  • Pulpal and periodontal complications
  • Need for radiographic assessment
  • Follow-up of traumatized teeth

Behavior Management in Pediatric Dentistry

Successful pediatric dental treatment requires effective communication between the dentist, child and parent or caregiver.

Basic Approaches

  • Tell-show-do
  • Positive reinforcement
  • Age-appropriate communication
  • Distraction
  • Desensitization
  • Parental guidance
  • Appropriate appointment planning

Behavior management should be individualized according to the child's developmental stage, anxiety, treatment requirements and safety.

Patient and Parent Education

Parents and caregivers play an important role in maintaining children's oral health.

  • Begin oral hygiene early.
  • Use age-appropriate fluoride toothpaste.
  • Limit frequent sugary snacks and drinks.
  • Encourage healthy dietary habits.
  • Schedule regular dental examinations.
  • Seek dental care early when pain, swelling or trauma occurs.
  • Follow recommended preventive and treatment plans.

Frequently Asked Questions About Pedodontics

What is Pedodontics?

Pedodontics is the branch of dentistry focused on the oral health care of infants, children and adolescents.

When should a child first visit a dentist?

A child's first dental visit is generally recommended early in life, allowing assessment of oral health, caries risk, preventive needs and parental education.

Why are primary teeth important?

Primary teeth contribute to chewing, speech, aesthetics and maintenance of space for developing permanent teeth.

What is Early Childhood Caries?

Early Childhood Caries is dental caries occurring in young children and can progress rapidly without appropriate prevention and treatment.

What is a space maintainer?

A space maintainer is an appliance used when appropriate to help preserve available space following premature loss of a primary tooth.
