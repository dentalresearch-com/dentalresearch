Oral Surgery

What is Oral Surgery?

Oral surgery is a branch of dentistry concerned with the diagnosis, surgical management and supportive care of diseases, injuries and conditions affecting the teeth, jaws, oral cavity and surrounding structures.

Common areas of oral surgery include tooth extraction, management of impacted teeth, minor oral surgical procedures, oral infections, trauma, biopsy and management of postoperative complications.

Clinical Approach to Oral Surgery

1. Patient Assessment

A systematic assessment begins with medical and dental history, clinical examination and identification of factors that may influence the planned surgical procedure.

2. Diagnosis and Treatment Planning

The clinical findings should be combined with appropriate investigations when necessary to establish a diagnosis and develop an appropriate treatment plan.

3. Infection Control

Standard infection-control principles and appropriate aseptic techniques are essential during oral surgical procedures.

4. Surgical Procedure

The surgical approach depends on the diagnosis, anatomical factors, patient-related considerations and complexity of the procedure.

5. Postoperative Care

Patients should receive appropriate postoperative instructions, including advice regarding oral hygiene, diet, medications when indicated and warning signs that require professional review.

Research and Evidence-Based Oral Surgery

Modern oral surgery should be guided by appropriate clinical evidence, patient-specific factors and accepted principles of diagnosis and treatment planning.

Research in oral surgery includes clinical trials, systematic reviews, observational studies, case reports and studies evaluating surgical techniques, biomaterials, pain management, infection control and patient outcomes.

Patient Education

Patients undergoing oral surgical procedures should understand their condition, the purpose of treatment, expected recovery and possible risks or complications. Clear postoperative instructions can help patients participate effectively in their recovery.
