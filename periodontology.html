What is Periodontology?

Periodontology is the branch of dentistry concerned with the study, prevention, diagnosis and management of diseases affecting the periodontium and supporting structures of the teeth.

The periodontium includes the gingiva, periodontal ligament, cementum and alveolar bone. Understanding these tissues is essential for maintaining periodontal health and preventing tooth-supporting tissue destruction.

Clinical Approach to Periodontal Disease

1. Patient History

A periodontal assessment begins with medical and dental history, oral hygiene practices, lifestyle factors, previous periodontal treatment and relevant risk factors.

2. Clinical Examination

Clinical examination may include assessment of gingival appearance, probing depth, clinical attachment level, bleeding on probing, tooth mobility and furcation involvement.

3. Radiographic Assessment

Radiographs may provide information about alveolar bone levels and other findings that contribute to diagnosis and treatment planning.

4. Diagnosis and Risk Assessment

Clinical findings, history and appropriate investigations are considered together to establish a diagnosis and assess factors that may influence periodontal disease progression.

5. Treatment Planning

Periodontal treatment is planned according to disease severity, patient-specific risk factors, oral hygiene status and clinical needs.

Periodontal Treatment

Oral Hygiene Measures

Patient education and effective plaque-control practices are important components of periodontal disease prevention and management.

Scaling

Scaling is performed to remove plaque and calculus deposits from tooth surfaces.

Root Surface Instrumentation

Instrumentation of affected root surfaces may be used as part of non-surgical periodontal therapy when clinically indicated.

Periodontal Surgery

Selected patients may require periodontal surgical procedures when non-surgical treatment alone does not adequately address the clinical condition.

Supportive Periodontal Therapy

Regular periodontal maintenance and monitoring are important for long-term periodontal health and prevention of disease recurrence.

Research and Evidence-Based Periodontology

Modern periodontology incorporates evidence from clinical research, epidemiological studies, systematic reviews and other scientific literature to improve prevention, diagnosis and treatment of periodontal diseases.

Research areas include periodontal microbiology, host response, risk factors, periodontal regeneration, treatment outcomes, implant-related diseases and supportive periodontal therapy.

Patient Education

Good oral hygiene and regular dental evaluation are important for maintaining periodontal health. Patients should understand the importance of plaque control, professional dental care and periodontal maintenance when recommended by their dental professional.
